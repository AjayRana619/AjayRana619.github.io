Getting There

Most international flights land at Narita (NRT) or Haneda (HND). Haneda is far closer to central Tokyo. Get a Suica IC card at the airport machine immediately — load ¥5,000 and use it everywhere for the entire trip.

Narita Express (NEX) to Shinjuku¥3,070 · ~60 min
Limousine Bus from Narita¥3,200 · ~90 min
Haneda Monorail to Hamamatsucho¥500 · ~30 min
Taxi from Haneda (city center)¥5,000–¥8,000
Tokyo · Days 1–4

The Megacity

Tokyo is not one city — it's 40 neighborhoods sharing a train network. Don't try to see everything. Pick your districts and go deep.

Transit

Tokyo → Kyoto by Shinkansen

The Nozomi bullet train covers the distance in 2 hours 15 minutes. It is spotless, silent, and on-time to the minute.

Tokyo → Kyoto (Nozomi, one-way)¥13,000–¥14,000
7-day JR Pass¥80,000
Honest JR Pass tip: For this specific Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka route, individual tickets cost roughly ¥28,000 return. The ¥80,000 JR Pass is not worth it unless you're also adding Hiroshima, Hakone, or other cities. Buy individual tickets via the JR West website.
Kyoto · Days 5–7

The Ancient Capital

Kyoto is the antidote to Tokyo — ancient, walkable, and genuinely peaceful if you time it right. Arrive at every famous site at opening time (8:00–9:00 AM). The difference between 8:30 and 10:30 is thousands of people.

Day 5

  • Iwatayama Monkey Park — spectacular city views from the top ¥600
  • Tenryu-ji Temple & Zen garden ¥500–¥800
  • Togetsukyo Bridge walk · matcha soft serve ¥400
Honest tip: The bamboo grove photos you've seen were taken at 6–7 AM. By 9:30 AM it's shoulder-to-shoulder tourists. Wake up early — it's genuinely worth it.

Nara · Day 8

The Deer Park

Nara is a day trip from Kyoto or Osaka. Small, ancient, and completely overrun with semi-wild deer that will bow at you, headbutt you, and steal your snacks without remorse. It is wonderful.

Day 8 — Full Day
Deer, Temples & Ancient Calm
  • Train from Kyoto ¥720 · 40 min or from Osaka ¥820 · 45 min
  • Nara Park — free-roaming deer everywhere Free
  • Shika senbei (deer crackers) — prepare to be completely surrounded ¥200
  • Todai-ji Temple — world's largest wooden building, giant Buddha inside ¥800
  • Kasuga Taisha Shrine Free–¥500
  • Wakakusa Mountain hike — panoramic views of the city ¥150
  • Return to Osaka for the evening
Honest deer tip: They are genuinely persistent. Keep all food inside your bag until you're ready. They will pull maps, tickets, and even wallets from your pockets thinking it might be food. Hilarious in hindsight.
Osaka · Days 9–10

The Kitchen of Japan

Osaka is where you eat. Repeatedly. The city's unofficial motto is kuidaore — eat until you drop — and locals take this very seriously. It's also Japan's most casually friendly city; strangers will actually start conversations with you here.

Day 9
Dotonbori & the Street Food Rampage
  • Takoyaki (octopus balls) at Dotonbori ¥500–¥700 for 6
  • Okonomiyaki (savory pancake) ¥800–¥1,200
  • Osaka Castle & surrounding park ¥600 castle / Park free
  • Shinsaibashi shopping street
  • Dotonbori at night — neon lights, the Glico Man, pure sensory overload Free
Honest tip: The best takoyaki is not from the big flashy Dotonbori stalls. Look for smaller side-street stands in Namba with queues of locals. Same price, noticeably better quality.

Honest Travel Tips

💴 Cash is still king

Many restaurants, temples, and local shops are cash-only. Carry ¥10,000–¥20,000 at all times. Use 7-Eleven, Lawson, or FamilyMart ATMs — they always accept international cards.

🚉 Get a Suica card first

Load ¥5,000 at the airport machine. Use it on every train, bus, and convenience store in every city. Tap in, tap out — no tickets, no queues, no stress whatsoever.

⏰ Arrive everywhere early

Fushimi Inari, the Bamboo Grove, Kinkaku-ji — arrive at opening (8–9 AM). The difference between 8:30 and 10:30 is thousands of tourists. Early mornings are when Japan is at its best.

🍱 Lunch sets are the secret

Restaurants charging ¥2,500 for dinner often serve the exact same food as a lunch set for ¥1,000–¥1,500. Always eat your main meal at midday in Japan.

🗑️ There are no public bins

Almost none, anywhere. You carry your trash until you find a convenience store. Don't be the tourist leaving rubbish on temple grounds — it's genuinely frowned upon.

🗺️ Trust Google Maps completely

Japanese transit on Google Maps is flawless — exact platform numbers, transfer times, live delays. Download offline maps for Tokyo, Kyoto, and Osaka before you land.
